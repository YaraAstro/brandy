# Discovery and positioning

Use with `discover` or to repair a missing brief. R1 §1.1 contributes the business-first and keyword exercises (`REPORT-H`). The decision framework below is `PRACTICE`.

## Establish the decision

Identify whether the project is a launch, repositioning, merger, extension, or refresh. Record the change desired, what must remain recognizable, who decides, budget/time constraints, deliverable boundaries, languages/markets, and the top three actual use contexts. A founder's preference is a constraint or hypothesis, not audience evidence. Reuse prior research instead of asking the same questions again.

Ask what customers hire the business to accomplish; what the business can substantiate; why alternatives fail; which audience matters first; and where identity currently creates friction. Avoid requesting confidential unit economics unless a decision needs them. A quick concept exercise may use explicit assumptions; delivery cannot silently treat assumptions as approvals.

## Build a usable strategy

Separate business objective (for example recognition in retail), design task (for example hierarchy on a narrow label), and evaluation method (for example identify brand and variant at printed size). A logo cannot by itself demonstrate increased sales, loyalty, or trust. Agree measures and baseline before claiming improvement.

Construct positioning as: for [audience + situation], [brand] is [frame of reference] that [specific value], supported by [credible proof], compared with [real alternative]. Treat this as an internal decision tool, not automatically public copy. Reject generic attributes unless expressed in a useful tradeoff: “clear under pressure” has more design implications than “modern.”

Limit creative criteria to a workable set, each with observable consequences. Example: “calm under operational pressure” → restrained hierarchy, explicit status labels, low visual clutter → evaluate in a real task. List anti-goals and equity to preserve. Where client goals conflict, document the tradeoff and decision owner.

## Exit artifact

Fill [intake](../templates/intake-brief.md) and [strategy](../templates/strategy.md). Mark facts, hypotheses, evidence gaps, approved decisions, and next tests. A sufficient discovery output contains audience priorities, differentiated value with proof, voice direction, application constraints, creative criteria, and a justified path to concepts. Missing evidence may be an assigned follow-up rather than a reason to stop all exploration. Commitments, legal contracts, and rights transfers remain outside this skill's inferred authority.
