# Audience and cultural interpretation

R1 §1.2 and R2 §1–3 motivate audience-specific choices (`REPORT-H`). Their “tribe” archetypes and claims about innate triggers are not established findings. Use behavior and situation rather than stereotypes.

## Segment by decision-relevant needs

Record buyer, user, recommender, and gatekeeper separately. A procurement manager, retail shopper, and daily software operator can encounter the same identity in different tasks. Describe situation, goal, alternatives, barriers, literacy/language, device/channel, accessibility needs, and relevant local conventions. Age or income alone does not imply a typeface, color, or shape preference.

Create an evidence table: question → source/sample → observation → interpretation → design implication → confidence. Interview notes and analytics are supplied evidence with limitations; AI-generated personas are hypotheses, never synthetic “research participants.” Respect privacy and consent, minimize personal data in project files, and do not infer sensitive attributes from appearance or browsing stereotypes.

## Learn without leading

Ask participants what they think the business does, what appears most important, what the tone suggests, what feels familiar or confusing, and what they would do next. Avoid “does this circle make you feel safe?” Show comparable stimuli, rotate concept order, and record exact wording before interpretation. Have participants from intended cultures/scripts evaluate symbolism and pronunciation; do not appoint an unrelated audience as a universal proxy.

For a small formative study, agree recruitment and task in advance, note sample size and relevant experience, and report counts/quotes with limits rather than invented statistical certainty. Measure recognition, comprehension, or task completion separately from preference. An identity liked by a stakeholder can still fail a navigation or labeling task.

## Turn learning into decisions

Example `HYPOTHESIS`: a repeated joining seam may express coordination to small production teams. Test unprompted interpretation and confusion with competitors in context. If responses conflict, inspect audience groups and application differences before averaging away the issue. Design variants can resolve functional differences; they should not become stereotyped identities for demographic groups.

Update the strategy evidence log with who was actually consulted, dates, stimuli versions, what changed, and what remains uncertain. Human testing is not claimed unless it occurred. No psychological outcome is guaranteed by the brief, a heuristic, or an automated render.
